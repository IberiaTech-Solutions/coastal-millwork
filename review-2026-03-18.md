# Coastal Millwork & Supply — Site Review
**Date:** 2026-03-18
**Perspective:** Architect / Millwork Engineer

---

## What Works Well

**Credibility signals are front and center.** AWI-QCP Premium certification, 800+ projects, 20+ years — that's the first thing I'd look for. The project gallery with contract values ($1M–$20M+ tiers) and named GCs/architects builds trust fast. Showing Spartanburg County Judicial Center at $5.7M or Charleston Airport at $3.7M tells me you can handle scale.

**The 6-step process page is strong.** Design → Value Engineering → Submittals → Production → PM → Install maps exactly to how I'd evaluate a millwork partner's workflow. The scroll-spy navigation (ServiceRail) makes it easy to jump between steps. This is the page that would keep me on the site.

**Sector breadth is impressive.** 16 sectors from aviation to senior living, backed by real project data — that tells an architect "these guys have seen my project type before."

**"One scope, one team" messaging is the right pitch.** Architects and GCs hate coordinating 4 separate subs for millwork, countertops, finishes, and install. Positioning as a single-source solution is the strongest differentiator here.

---

## What an Architect/Engineer Would Want More Of

**Shop drawing / submittal examples.** Engineers live in the details. A sample submittal package or shop drawing excerpt (even redacted) would be more convincing than any copy. Consider a "Resources" or "Technical" section with:
- Sample shop drawing PDFs
- Typical detail sections (reveals, edge profiles, panel joints)
- Material spec sheets or finish samples

**Specification guidance.** Architects writing specs (CSI Division 06 / 12) want to know: "Can I spec to AWI Premium and trust this shop to deliver?" A downloadable spec template or guide section ("How to specify Coastal Millwork") would be a differentiator most competitors don't offer.

**Project case studies with technical depth.** The gallery is good for browsing, but each project page could benefit from:
- Challenges solved (e.g., "curved veneer radius under 24", sequenced install around active hospital wing")
- Materials/species used
- Timeline and phasing info
- Before/during/after photos showing install progression

**Microvellum / CAD-CAM callout could be stronger.** You mention Microvellum in passing — for an engineer, that's a signal of serious CNC integration and shop drawing accuracy. Consider showing a render-to-reality comparison.

**Lead times / capacity info.** A GC building a schedule wants to know: "What's your typical shop drawing turnaround? What's your production capacity? Can you handle concurrent projects?" Even ballpark ranges would help.

---

## Bottom Line

The site presents well and hits the right notes for a commercial audience. It's clearly built by someone who understands the industry, not just web design. The biggest gap is **technical depth** — architects and engineers want to see that you speak their language at the detail level (tolerances, species, joinery, finish systems), not just at the marketing level. Adding a technical resources section and deeper case studies would move this from "looks professional" to "I'm calling them Monday."
